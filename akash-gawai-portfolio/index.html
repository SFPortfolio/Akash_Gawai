04 / HOW I THINK

Good work
leaves a clear trail.

A resolved issue is more useful when someone else can understand what happened. That’s why documentation is part of my engineering process.

● ● ●WORKING NOTES / AKASH GAWAI↗
01 / INCIDENT NOTE

Start with the symptom.
Leave with a next step.

What is happening?
Describe the user’s action, expected result, and observed result.
What did we check?
Capture reproduction steps, affected users, relevant logs, and configuration changes.
What happens next?
Record the resolution or workaround, validation, owner, and follow-up.
02 / AUTOMATION PLAN

Understand the process.
Then design the Flow.

What should trigger it?
Write down the business event, entry criteria, and intended outcome.
Where could it go wrong?
Consider missing data, permissions, duplicate execution, and fault handling.
How will we validate it?
Agree on normal, exception, and bulk scenarios before moving into UAT.
03 / RELEASE HANDOFF

Make the change clear.
Make the next step easy.

What is changing?
Explain the business purpose, affected functionality, and deployment components.
What has been checked?
Summarize test results, UAT feedback, dependencies, and known limitations.
Who takes it forward?
Document the support owner, validation steps, and agreed recovery plan.
